I came to research through the back door, and I think it made me better at it.

Most researchers start with the title. I started by noticing that the same questions kept showing up in support tickets at a healthcare SaaS company. I wrote up the patterns, proposed documentation that addressed them, and watched duplicate tickets drop by 18%. It took me a while to realize that what I'd done was research.

Six months into a frontline role at a shared housing marketplace, that same instinct has produced four substantial bodies of work — a host enablement framework, a stakeholder-risk rebalancing study, a maintenance triage tool, and a research methodology I'm now formalizing through a UX research internship and degree completion in August.

The thread across all of it: I'm drawn to systems where the user experience extends past the screen — where the real friction lives in policy gaps, operational decisions, stakeholder tradeoffs, and the spaces between handoffs. Two-sided marketplaces, service operations, trust and safety. Places where research has to account for real consequences for real people.

Host Enablement Framework

Reducing post-activation churn on a shared housing marketplace through structured user enablement aligned to lifecycle moments.

Role
Sole researcher & content designer
Timeline
6 weeks, self-directed
Methods
Support ticket synthesis, thematic analysis, journey mapping, content design
Output
Three lifecycle-aligned enablement guides, proposed 90-day outreach cadence

Context

A shared housing marketplace activates hundreds of new hosts per month with no defined post-activation communication journey. Frontline support data suggested most new-host frustration clustered in the first 30 days around the same handful of issues: confusing booking-fee structures, unclear pricing strategy, weak listing visibility, and uncertainty about member screening within fair-housing constraints. Hosts churning early was costing the platform supply growth.

Research Question

What are the recurring friction points new hosts hit in their first 90 days on the platform, and how can structured enablement content address them before they become churn signals?

Process

Pattern identification

Reviewed recurring themes across 60 days of host support tickets. Grouped tickets by topic (pricing, photos, screening, booking flow, payments, member relations) and noted which themes consistently appeared in the first 30 days of a host's lifecycle.

Thematic synthesis

Clustered the most common friction points into four core themes:

  • Expectation gap — new hosts expected immediate bookings and were surprised by the visibility ramp
  • Earnings confusion — the booking-fee structure, 8% service fee, and commitment-break penalty rules were poorly understood
  • Listing optimization — hosts didn't know which levers actually drove booking volume (photos, pricing, description quality, ratings)
  • Screening uncertainty — hosts wanted to vet members but didn't know what they could legally ask under fair-housing law

Solution mapping

Mapped each theme to a content deliverable, and grouped those deliverables by host lifecycle stage:

  • Week 1–2 (new host) — What to Expect as a New Host: sets visibility ramp expectations, explains booking fees and timelines, introduces proactive habits
  • Month 1+ (active host, low bookings) — Increase Member Bookings: listing fundamentals audit, pricing analysis, screening form design within fair-housing constraints, referral program walkthrough
  • Month 3+ (established host, optimization) — How to Boost Your Listing: description optimization, photo standards, pricing strategy, ratings management

Compliance review

Validated all screening guidance against fair-housing law to ensure recommendations didn't introduce legal exposure for hosts. Every screening question framework was vetted against protected-class categories.

Outcomes

  • Three companion guides covering the post-activation journey end-to-end
  • Proposed 90-day automated outreach cadence aligning each piece of content to a host lifecycle moment
  • Recommendation to engineering and product to track open rates, time-to-first-booking, and 60-day retention as the success metrics

Reflection

The biggest lesson was that frontline tickets are an underused research dataset. Most of the friction patterns I documented had been visible in the queue for months — they just weren't being aggregated into actionable themes.

If I ran it again, I would add 8 to 10 semi-structured interviews with hosts who churned in the first 60 days to triangulate ticket data against firsthand accounts. The risk of ticket-only research is that you only hear from hosts who reached out — silent churners often have the most important story.
